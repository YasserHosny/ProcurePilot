# Lean Startup Circle – Egypt: company-setup posts and comments (detailed analysis)

Source: Facebook group "Lean Startup Circle – Egypt", posts found via the group's own search and read in full by a browser-driven session on 2026-10-17.
Language of the source: Egyptian Arabic. Everything below is a translation or paraphrase.

## Method and limits (read first)

- This is **anecdotal peer advice from a social group**, not legal or tax guidance. Every figure and legal claim is as stated by a commenter and **unverified**.
- Per-post page text was captured up to about 5–19 KB, so **long comment threads are cut off** (the cut-offs are marked "thread truncated"). Replies hidden behind "view more replies" were not expanded.
- Posters and commenters are deliberately **not named** and phone numbers are omitted. Posts are cited by their Facebook post ID: `facebook.com/groups/LeanStartupCircleEgypt/posts/<id>`.
- Nine posts were captured. The earlier session also ran searches ("flip delaware", one-person company, e-invoicing) and was interrupted by a usage limit before it could read every hit, so **coverage is partial**.

## Post-by-post

### 1. One-person software company: cost, USD receipts, taxes (`4447160388858851`)
**Question:** cost to set up a one-person company (builds and sells software, one-off and monthly subscription); can it receive USD; is an accountant required or can the founder self-file (heard about "lump-sum estimated" tax assessment); how much tax.
**Answers:**
- Cost depends on capital and activity; tax is on annual profit per the balance sheet.
- Minimum capital for a one-person company is stated as **EGP 1,000**, deposited before registration.
- One commenter: avoid one-person companies, take a partner (even a relative) and form an LLC so liability sits with the company.
- The poster pushed back: individual establishments carry personal liability, a one-person *company* does not. Another commenter agreed: it is a capital company ("شركة أموال"), not a sole proprietorship.
- Questions on USD receipts and the accountant were not answered in the captured part. Thread truncated.

### 2. One-person company vs LLC: timeline, cost, advisors (`4211721065736119`)
**Question:** how long to set up each; which is better for a sole owner; registering via the investment authority (GAFI) for tax-facilitation benefits; business is online courses for Gulf students; what capital; is a physical office needed or a virtual one; accountant or lawyer; how to pick a firm that also handles returns and e-invoicing afterwards.
**Answers:**
- Mostly **service-provider self-promotion** (accounting/legal firms, WhatsApp numbers).
- Timeline given: **1–2 months** depending on the authority and tax office; the poster had heard ~20 days.
- Personal recommendations of specific firms by two commenters. Thread truncated.

### 3. Freelancer-turned-software-company: legal form, papers, costs (`4351712171737007`)
**Question:** freelancer (online stores, apps, CRM) wants a formal company; sole vs LLC; documents, licences, rough cost; tax and social-insurance items; what is needed to contract with companies.
**Answers:**
- An **LLC is preferable**; cost depends on capital.
- Reason given: some **government bodies deal only with companies**, and an LLC lets partners join later.
- Clarification: a "general partnership" cannot have one person; the real choice is one-person company vs LLC, and "there is no big difference between them".
- Lots of offers of services and job-seeking by developers. Thread truncated.

### 4. Freelancer losing clients for lack of commercial register, tax card, e-invoice (`4265032827071609`)
**Situation:** earns about EGP 100k a year with sporadic clients, works alone.
**Answers:**
- One commenter estimates **EGP 10–15k** to set up; an LLC needs at least one other partner; capital need not actually be deposited but fees and VAT obligations apply; an office is needed from year two (home is fine in year one).
- Strong advice **not to incorporate yet**: a company needs 6–7 months of liquidity and enough clients to cover personal income; do it last, once companies are asking for a product.
- Counter: a one-person company works without a partner and is also a capital company.
- Reply: a sole-entity register with no team may make buyers doubt the business.
- Another commenter suggested the **new entrepreneurship ("ريادة الأعمال") register**. Thread truncated.

### 5. Delaware incorporation: what are the downsides? (`3462187017356198`)
**Answers:**
- Two users report **no problems** (one for five years): payments, bank account, address and phone all work.
- "**No advantage if your market is American**" and "you pay a lot of tax".
- Annual renewal for a Delaware LLC cited at **about USD 350**.
- A commenter explains Delaware is a US state and that an LLC can be formed online without an Egyptian branch.
- Most of the thread is "interested / DM me" replies. Thread long (~19 KB captured), cost and bank questions mostly unanswered publicly.

### 6. FinTech/SaaS for global customers: Egypt LLC vs Delaware (`4450021865239370`)
**Question:** has anyone used Law 52 of 2020 (start-up incentives law) or the ministerial group's tax facilitation; is a US entity cheaper long-term.
**Answers:**
- "If you sell in Egypt you must incorporate in Egypt. If you sell globally **and** in Egypt you need both a US and an Egyptian entity, because there are **restrictions on payments from inside Egypt when collected via a gateway like Stripe**." The commenter is a formation-service vendor.
- Warning: a US entity must file annual tax returns; missing them or a law change can bring a **USD 25,000 penalty** (stated, unverified).
- Recommendation of a US law firm. The Law 52 and incentives questions were **not answered** in the captured part.

### 7. LLC in the US via Stripe Atlas – first-hand walkthrough (`4061149624126598`)
Founder of an Egyptian startup shares his experience:
- **Stripe Atlas**, about **USD 500** (≈ EGP 26,000 at the time) plus about **USD 100 per year**; **3–4 working days**.
- Needs a valid passport and ID, personal and company details, and a website. A Delaware registered address is included.
- Next step is a **Mercury** bank account (approved in 1–2 working days), then link Stripe.
- Payout to an Egyptian bank is indirect: Stripe → Mercury → Egypt, at a **flat USD 15 or about 1–3%**.
- Shares can be distributed to stakeholders at the start while it is an LLC.
- One commenter asked about taxes when serving US territory; answer: depends on Delaware LLC rules.

### 8. Payment gateways for cross-border services (`4282114648696760`)
**Problem (healthcare cross-border startup):** (1) all Egyptian gateways contacted settle **only in EGP** even for export-of-services; (2) they won't split payments between the company and its vendors/partners; (3) customer financing is awkward for non-Egyptian customers.
**Answers:**
- "Stripe" – then "the foreign-entity problem": "just set up a company".
- Another: **no Egyptian payment company collects in anything but EGP, by central-bank rule** (as far as they know); the realistic route is direct transfers to a foreign-currency bank account without a gateway.
- Poster's reply was cut off. Thread truncated.

### 9. One-person company for a SaaS property-management platform (`4390607884514102`)
**Question:** cost and time; poster states capital will be EGP 1,000; demands direct answers, not questions.
**Answers:** quotes of **EGP 5,000** and **EGP 15,000 + capital deposit** (the deposit "can be 1,000, or any amount"); fees vary by activity and by regular vs VIP service; a service provider objects to quoting fees publicly and says setup takes **about two weeks** with complete papers. Thread truncated.

## Cross-cutting themes

| Theme | What the group says | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum capital, one-person company | EGP 1,000 (deposit before registration) | Repeated by 2 posts, unverified |
| Setup cost in Egypt | EGP 5k–15k + capital deposit (professional fees) | 3 quotes, vendor-sourced |
| Setup time in Egypt | ~2 weeks (complete papers) to 1–2 months | Mixed |
| Legal form | LLC and one-person company both capital companies with limited liability; no sole proprietorships for B2B credibility | Majority view |
| Getting paid in USD | Egyptian gateways settle in EGP only; foreign entity or direct bank transfer needed | Consistent across 3 posts |
| US route | Stripe Atlas ≈ USD 500 + ~100/yr, Mercury bank, payout 1–3% or USD 15; Delaware annual ≈ USD 350 | One detailed first-hand report |
| US downside | Ongoing US filings and penalties, tax exposure, no benefit if market is not American | Few, anecdotal |
| Advisors | Heavy vendor presence; trust built by DM; public fee quoting disliked | Observed |
| Timing | Several advise delaying incorporation until there is steady demand | One strong opinion |

## What was not covered

Law 52 of 2020 benefits, e-invoicing mechanics, and tax rates received no substantive answers in the captured text. Searches for "flip Delaware" and further e-invoicing posts were started but not read.
