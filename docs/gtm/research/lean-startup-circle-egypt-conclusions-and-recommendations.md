# Conclusions and recommendations for a new Egyptian-founded B2B SaaS (ProcurePilot context)

Based on `lean-startup-circle-egypt-company-setup-analysis.md`. Treat everything as **hypotheses to confirm with a licensed Egyptian lawyer/accountant and a US tax adviser**: the evidence is peer anecdote, partly vendor-sourced, with truncated threads.

## Conclusions

1. **Incorporate as a capital company, not a sole proprietorship.** Both a one-person company and an LLC give limited liability; commenters consistently say an LLC reads as more credible to buyers and government bodies, and eases adding partners/investors later. For a procurement product sold to businesses, buyer trust matters.
2. **Egyptian setup is cheap and fast enough.** Reported EGP 5–15k plus a capital deposit (as low as EGP 1,000) and roughly 2 weeks to 2 months. It is not a reason to delay or to run to a foreign entity.
3. **Getting paid in foreign currency is the real constraint.** All reported Egyptian gateways settle in EGP only. This does not hurt an EGP-priced Egyptian product (ProcurePilot's home currency is EGP) but blocks USD-priced global SaaS billed through local gateways.
4. **A US entity is a sequencing decision, not a default.** Stripe Atlas (≈ USD 500 + ~100/yr) + Mercury works in days, but payout to Egypt is indirect (USD 15 or 1–3%), brings annual US filing duties with reported heavy penalties, and gives "no advantage if your market is not American".
5. **Don't incorporate before demand exists** (one strong view): wait for repeat inbound demand rather than one or two clients.
6. **Advisor market is noisy.** Most replies are solicitations; pick advisers by referral, and compare written fee quotes.

## Recommendations

| # | Action | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Form an Egyptian LLC or one-person company through GAFI **once there is at least one paying pilot** | Buyer credibility, e-invoicing/tax card requirement raised in posts 3 and 4 |
| 2 | Price and bill Egyptian customers in **EGP** via local gateway/bank; keep this consistent with the product's home-currency design | Matches the gateway reality |
| 3 | Defer a US entity until **foreign-currency customers or US investors exist**; then evaluate Stripe Atlas + Mercury, with a US tax adviser | Avoids filing penalties and fixed cost early |
| 4 | Ask your accountant specifically about **Law 52/2020 start-up incentives** and **e-invoicing onboarding** | Posts raised these but got no real answers |
| 5 | Choose a firm that **continues after setup** (returns, e-invoices) and get fees in writing | Poster's own requirement in post 2 |
| 6 | Use this group for **customer discovery**, not just formation advice: founders here repeatedly cite payments, invoicing, and vendor management pain | Product signal |
| 7 | Re-run the research with the remaining unread posts and an expanded view of long threads before relying on it | Coverage was partial |

## Product signals relevant to ProcurePilot

- E-invoicing and a commercial register/tax card are **gating requirements for winning B2B clients** (post 4): reinforces e-invoice/accounting integration (spec 014).
- Split payments between a company and its vendors were wanted and unsupported by gateways (post 8): note as a possible future workflow; no purchasing automation without human authorisation (constitution rule 8).
- Founders prize **direct, written fee transparency** and dislike DM-only quoting, which is consistent with ProcurePilot's comparable-quotes value proposition.
