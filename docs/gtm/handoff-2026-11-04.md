## 1. Vision

**Vision.** Every small business should buy as well as a large enterprise, with the same price visibility, supplier leverage and decision evidence, without the cost, staff or complexity.

**Mission.** Turn fragmented supplier information into trusted, comparable purchasing decisions, and prove the money saved. Three verbs define the product:
1. **Normalise**: make offers genuinely comparable (product identity, pack size, VAT, delivery, terms).
2. **Recommend**: turn the comparison into a specific, evidence-backed action.
3. **Verify**: measure the realised saving, so value is proven, not claimed.

**The problem, commercially.** Small and mid-size buyers lose money in small, invisible, repeated ways: quotes arrive as WhatsApp photos, nobody compares every supplier on every item, units differ (carton vs. kilo), branches order without approval, and "we saved money" is a feeling.

**What we will never do** (constitution rule 8): buy autonomously. Automation prepares and routes; a person authorises every purchase.

Full vision, phases and stage gates: `docs/roadmap/procurepilot_roadmap.md` (§1–3). Binding engineering rules: `.specify/memory/constitution.md`.

## 2. Who we sell to first (ICP)

- **Market**: Egypt first; Saudi Arabia second (GCC); wider Africa later. Arabic-first, with Arabic and English throughout.
- **Buyer**: multi-branch F&B groups (restaurants, cafés, bakeries, cloud kitchens), 2–40 outlets, that run purchasing on WhatsApp, Excel and phone calls today.
- **Pilot qualification** (all five): 2+ branches (or 1 + central kitchen); 5+ suppliers with weekly orders; quotes collected by hand; one named decision-maker for two 30-min check-ins; will share last month's prices on 10–20 items.
- **Disqualify**: franchisees of global brands (the franchisor sets suppliers); 40+ outlets with central procurement or an ERP; single-site owner-buyers.
- **The main competitor is the status quo** (WhatsApp + Excel). Named alternatives: Supy (GCC inventory), Suplyd (Egypt marketplace), Foodics (POS, likely an integration partner). See `docs/gtm/competitor-profiles/_summary.md`.

**Positioning line** (AR first): «خلّي الموردين يبعتوا على الواتساب زي ما هم. إحنا بس بنحط العروض جنب بعض قبل ما تطلب، وبنقولك وفّرت كام بالجنيه.» / "Keep your suppliers on WhatsApp. We put their quotes side by side before you order, and tell you what you saved."

**Commercial terms (owner decision D-2, 2026-10-14):** free 14-day pilot (clock starts after onboarding and the first live RFQ); then EGP 1,500 per branch per month, minimum 2 branches; annual = 10 months' price; founding customers 50% off for 12 months for a case study and logo; if verified savings in a month are under 3× the fee, the next month is free. **KSA has no price yet**: offer the free trial only.

## 3. The product today

A FastAPI modular monolith (`apps/api`), an Angular 22 web app (`apps/web`), a Flutter mobile app (`apps/mobile`), Supabase Postgres with row-level security, Redis + RQ workers, and two separately deployed workers (`services/extraction-worker`, `services/optimiser`). Stack and commands: `CLAUDE.md`.

**Shipped** (every release R1.0–R4.3 has a Speckit spec in `specs/` and is merged; specs 001–021, 023 and 024 are complete; 022 has three tasks open):

| The flow a buyer runs | What exists |
|---|---|
| Send an RFQ to several suppliers at once | Yes; sending is always a person's action. Cancel an RFQ (#475/#476, 2026-11-04) |
| Quotes come back | By email (Mailgun inbound) or upload. **No direct WhatsApp connection**: the buyer forwards or uploads the photo/PDF |
| Extraction | Arabic and English quotes, Arabic-Indic digits, each line linked to its place in the document, reviewed by a person. Accuracy is **not measured** (no held-out benchmark) |
| Matching to the catalogue | RFQ replies match the RFQ's own lines (spec 023); decisions are corrected by supersession, never updated (ADR-022) |
| Compare | Line by line, pack for pack, delivery included; basket split across suppliers (spec 024, one request group approved as one, ADR-023) |
| Guardrails | Optional: the winning reply is prepared as a **draft** purchase request |
| Approve | Web and phone; the phone approves **requests**, not purchase orders |
| Purchase order | Generated on the web from the approved request; receipts; print and CSV export |
| Savings | A saving counts only once someone verifies its evidence (savings ledger) |

**Known gaps (say them honestly on calls):** no WhatsApp integration; no Foodics/Odoo integration (QuickBooks, Xero and Square exist but don't matter in Egypt); one currency per comparison (no FX, gap C4); push notifications not delivered on hosted; extraction accuracy unmeasured; match-score calibration impossible until 500+ labelled decisions exist; several background workers (report schedules, exports, digests, webhooks, offer refresh) are not deployed on hosted (PPS-12).

**What can be claimed publicly**: only the approved claims C-01…C-17 in `marketing/ProcurePilot/knowledge/proof-points.md`. No savings figures, accuracy figures, customers, logos or "secure/certified". Demo numbers are always labelled as demo data.

## 9. Rules that bind every change

**Product (constitution and `CLAUDE.md` non-negotiables):** tenant isolation in the database (RLS `ENABLE` + `FORCE`, `USING` + `WITH CHECK`); tenancy from the verified JWT only; a cross-tenant read returns "not found"; no secrets in the repo (gitleaks blocks); every user-facing string from `packages/i18n` (en + ar); every money value carries its currency; `audit_event` is append-only; no autonomous purchasing. Quantities are in the purchasing pack (ADR-020). Migrations are forward-only and never edited after merge; deploys never run migrations.

**Marketing and sales copy:** approved claims only (C-01…C-17); never invent statistics, savings, testimonials or customers; label demo data; "WhatsApp" always comes with "you forward or upload"; nothing is posted, sent or published without the owner's approval; Arabic first, Egyptian register for Egypt, Gulf register for KSA.

**Public repo:** no named prospects, call notes, passwords, IPs or tokens in committed files. A security fix gets neutral commit and PR text; the review record is published only after deploy + migrate.

**Jira (`PPS`, Scrum, board 1; site and cloudId in `gtm-execution-plan.md` §1):** every piece of work has a PPS task with the plan ID in the summary (`[P-n] …`) and a row in the plan file's key map; a dated **Progress** comment on every change of state (what's done with PR numbers, what's left, who owns it); status = board column (To Do → In Progress → In Review when a PR is open → Done); dependencies as *Blocks* links. **Plan IDs collide between concurrent sessions**: check `origin/main`'s plan file and PPS titles for the latest P-n before taking one. The Atlassian connector often times out but still writes: re-read before retrying.

**Git and merging:** `main` is protected with required checks (including «Regenerate and diff» for docs screenshots). Branch from `origin/main`; only merge once the CI run **for the current head commit** has finished green (an empty check list after a push is not green). Demo-video media is in Git LFS. Before opening a PR, run `debate-review --local` (two-model review) and fix real findings.

**How the owner runs Claude sessions** (owner preference): Claude orchestrates, diagnoses, briefs and verifies; implementation goes to delegate lanes (Agy for web/design, Codex for backend, OpenCode as a local fallback), with tenancy/RLS work kept in house. Tests are written red first. Every chunk is driven end-to-end in the real UI, not only by its own tests.
