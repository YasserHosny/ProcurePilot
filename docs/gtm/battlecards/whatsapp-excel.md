# Battlecard: the status quo, WhatsApp and Excel

**Last verified**: 2026-10-30 · **First version** · **Jira**: PPS-114 (P-83)
**Competitor**: no vendor. The buyer's own way of working: suppliers send prices as WhatsApp photos or voice notes, someone types them into Excel (or nobody does), and the owner or purchasing manager picks a supplier from memory.

> Nothing on this card has been tested on a real call yet. Every "where we win" line is a **hypothesis**. Log what you hear on the SP Lead; update the card after 5 conversations.

## When you'll hear it
Almost every call. Expect it early, often as the first reply to the DM or email:
- «إحنا ماشيين كويس بالواتساب والإكسل» / "WhatsApp and Excel work fine for us."
- «الموردين بتوعنا متعودين على الواتساب» / "Our suppliers only do WhatsApp."
- «عندي حد مسؤول عن الموضوع ده» / "I have someone who handles that."

## Why it sticks (their real reasons, don't argue these)
- **It's free and everyone already has it.** Suppliers, branch managers and the owner are all on WhatsApp today.
- **Relationships live there.** The buyer has known these suppliers for years; the chat *is* the relationship.
- **It's fast for one order.** One message, one photo, done. Nobody has to learn anything.
- **Excel bends to anything.** The purchasing manager built the sheet and trusts it.

## What the status quo can't do (the gap we sell into)
| Pain (ask, don't assert) | Why WhatsApp + Excel can't fix it | What ProcurePilot does (approved claim) |
|---|---|---|
| Quotes are photos in different chats; comparing means retyping | Nobody compares every supplier on every item every week; they compare the 2–3 they remember | Reads the quotes you upload or forward, and puts them side by side pack for pack, delivery included (C-01, C-03) |
| Prices are "per carton" from one supplier and "per kilo" from another | Excel only compares what someone converted by hand | Compares pack for pack (C-03); quantities stay in the purchasing pack |
| Branches order on their own; the owner sees the bill later | A WhatsApp group has no approval step and no budget | Branch requests reach an approver with the remaining budget shown; approve from the phone (C-06, C-09) |
| "We saved money this month" is a feeling | Nobody records the price they *would* have paid | A saving counts only once someone verifies its evidence (C-07) |
| When the purchasing person is away, nobody knows the last prices | The history is in one person's phone | Every quote and decision stays in the workspace, not in one phone |

**Positioning line** (AR first):
«خلّي الموردين يبعتوا على الواتساب زي ما هم. إحنا بس بنحط العروض جنب بعض قبل ما تطلب، وبنقولك وفّرت كام بالجنيه.»
"Keep your suppliers on WhatsApp. We put their quotes side by side before you order, and tell you what you saved, in pounds."

## Where we can win, and for whom (hypotheses)
| Hypothesis | Who it fits | What would confirm it |
|---|---|---|
| Groups at 3+ branches lose track of which supplier was cheapest on which item | 3–15 branches, a purchasing or ops manager, 5+ suppliers ordered from weekly | They can't say last week's cheapest supplier for their top 5 items without opening chats |
| The owner wants control over branch spending without being on every order | Owner-run groups that just opened a branch (Tante, Sky Restaurants in Wave 1) | Owner says "I approve everything on WhatsApp" or "branches order by themselves" |
| Owners and finance want a savings number they can show someone | Investor-backed or expanding groups (Zooba, Sky) | They ask "how would I know it saved money?" |
| The purchasing manager is overloaded, not lazy | One person buying for many branches | They describe evenings spent on prices, or a backlog of quotes |

## Discovery questions (surface the cost without insulting their system)
Ask these before showing anything. Each has an Arabic version you can say as written.
1. «العروض بتوصلكم إزاي، ومين بيقارنها؟» / "How do quotes reach you, and who compares them?"
2. «آخر مرة غيرتوا مورد عشان السعر، ده حصل إمتى؟ وعرفتوا إزاي إنه أرخص؟» / "When did you last switch supplier on price? How did you know they were cheaper?"
3. «لو سألتك دلوقتي: مين أرخص مورد للزيت الأسبوع اللي فات؟ تعرف تقول من غير ما تفتح الواتساب؟» / "If I asked you now who was cheapest on oil last week, could you say without opening WhatsApp?"
4. «الفروع بتطلب لوحدها، ولا في حد بيوافق قبل الطلب؟» / "Do branches order on their own, or does someone approve first?"
5. «لو الشخص المسؤول عن الشراء أخد إجازة أسبوع، إيه اللي بيحصل؟» / "If your buyer took a week off, what would happen?"
6. «بتحسبوا التوفير؟ لو أيوه، إزاي؟» / "Do you track savings? If so, how?"

Listen for: a number of suppliers (5+), a number of hours, a story about a price nobody caught, a branch that ordered without asking. Write the buyer's exact words on the SP Lead.

We don't use it to train AI models, and if you don't continue, you get an export and we delete it." | C-17; pilot agreement (G-6). Don't say "secure" or "bank-grade". |

## When to walk away
- **One branch, owner buys everything personally, 2–3 suppliers.** WhatsApp is the right tool; we add steps.
- **They already compare every supplier every week** in a sheet that works, and spending is approved. Ask for a referral instead.
- **The pain is delivery or quality, not price.** We don't deliver goods, and we don't fix a late truck.
- **Suppliers quote in USD next to EGP** on the key items (imports). No exchange rates yet (gap C4); come back later.
- **40+ outlets with central procurement or an ERP.** Above pilot scale (Wave 1 disqualifier).

## Do not say
"You're losing X%" or any savings figure (no real data yet) · "Excel is outdated" or anything that mocks their system · "Works with WhatsApp" without "you forward or upload" in the same sentence · "Real-time alerts" (push notifications are not delivered to pilots yet) · "Secure", "certified", "bank-grade".
