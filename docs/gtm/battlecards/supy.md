# Battlecard: Supy

**Last verified**: 2026-10-28 (supy.io: homepage, pricing, supplier management, Supy Connect) · **First version**

## When you'll see them
Multi-branch F&B groups in the GCC, mostly in the UAE and KSA. Supy also has an Arabic site and named Saudi customers. Expect it in any group of 5–30 branches that has already "digitised the back of house".

## Why buyers pick them
"One system for stock, recipes, orders and invoices across all branches", with controls ("200+ customisable user permissions… approval workflows") and food-cost wins ("food cost has dropped from 39% to 33%", customer quote on supy.io).

## Where they're strong (don't argue these)
- Inventory, recipes, wastage, stock counts and COGS. **ProcurePilot has no recipe or food-cost module.**
- 50+ POS integrations, accounting push, and a central kitchen module
- Approvals, permissions, and POs sent to suppliers over email or **WhatsApp**
- GCC references (MAMA'ESH, 22 locations; Donk Group, Riyadh) and implementation in 2–4 weeks
- Price: "Start from $250/month"

## Where we can win, and for whom (hypotheses)
| Hypothesis | Who it fits | What would confirm it |
|---|---|---|
| Supy keeps a **price list per supplier** (bulk edit or Excel import). It doesn't help you *get* and *compare* competing quotes. | Buyers who re-quote often (produce, meat, packaging) or who have 3+ suppliers per category | Buyer confirms they compare quotes outside Supy (WhatsApp, Excel) |
| ProcurePilot turns PDF, photo and Arabic quotes into one comparable basket, and can split it across suppliers | Buyers whose suppliers send quotes as photos or PDFs | The buyer's own sample quote is extracted live in the demo |
| ProcurePilot records **savings per purchasing decision**; Supy reports food-cost % | Owners and CFOs who want proof that purchasing pays for itself | Buyer asks "how much did we save on that order?" and Supy can't answer it |

**Positioning line:** "Supy runs your stock. ProcurePilot picks your supplier, and shows the receipt for the saving."

## Discovery questions
1. "When the tomato price moves, how do you find out who's cheapest this week?"
2. "Where do supplier quotes arrive today, and who types them into Supy?"
3. "Last month, how many orders did you put out to more than one supplier?"
4. "If your owner asked 'how much did purchasing save us this quarter', where would that number come from?"

## Objections
| They say | Answer (one line) | Proof / next step |
|---|---|---|
| "Supy already does purchasing." | "It does the order after you've chosen. We help you choose: collect the quotes, compare them, then the PO goes out." | Demo: an Arabic photo quote → Smart Compare |
| "We don't want two systems." | "Fair. Then use us only for the categories you re-quote, e.g. produce. The pilot is 14 days on 10–20 items." | Pilot scope in `first-customer-gtm-plan.md` |
| "Supy has price-variance alerts." | "Those flag a price after it changes on an invoice. We get competing prices before you order." | Ask: "Did the alert ever tell you who was cheaper?" |
| "Does it connect to Supy?" | "Not today. Exports (CSV) are the hand-off during the pilot." | Log as a `from-pilot` gap if it blocks the deal |

## When to walk away
- The buyer's pain is **stock counts, recipes or food-cost %**, not supplier prices. Supy is the better fit.
- Single-supplier categories, or prices fixed by annual contract: there's nothing to compare.
- They need POS and inventory integration on day one.

## Do not say
"Supy can't do quotes" (we only know it wasn't on their site) · any Supy customer number as fact (their site says both 3,000+ and 3,500+).
