# Battlecard: Suplyd

**Last verified**: 2026-10-28 (suplyd.app homepage; MENAbytes 2025-08-04; Foodics release 2023-11-08) · **First version**

## When you'll see them
Egypt, everywhere. Suplyd claims "over 5,000 restaurants across Egypt" (MENAbytes, 2025). Expect "we order through Suplyd" from cafés and casual-dining groups in Cairo and Giza, and from Foodics users (the two are integrated).

## Why buyers pick them
"One app, 1,000+ products, next-day delivery, no chasing ten suppliers on WhatsApp." It's convenient, it has payment and financing options, and they say it's reliable ("100% successful order fulfillment rate", company claim).

## Where they're strong (don't argue these)
- They **deliver**. ProcurePilot doesn't sell or ship goods.
- One catalogue across categories, e-invoicing, e-payments, supply-chain financing
- Funded ($2M pre-Series A, Aug 2025), local team, Foodics integration
- Very little effort for a single-site restaurant

## What Suplyd is, in one line
A **marketplace**: a supplier that aggregates other suppliers. It is not the buyer's comparison tool.

## Where we can win, and for whom (hypotheses)
| Hypothesis | Who it fits | What would confirm it |
|---|---|---|
| Multi-branch groups keep **their own negotiated suppliers** for key categories and use Suplyd for top-ups | Groups of 3+ branches with a purchasing manager | Buyer names suppliers they buy from directly |
| Suplyd's price is **one quote** among several. ProcurePilot puts it next to the buyer's own suppliers. | Buyers who suspect convenience costs them money | Side by side, Suplyd's price is higher on 3+ key items in the buyer's basket |
| The buyer wants independent proof of savings, not the seller's own recommendations | Owners and finance | Buyer asks for a savings number for the owner or the bank |

**Positioning line:** "Keep Suplyd. Just put its price next to your other suppliers' before you order."

## Discovery questions
1. "What do you buy from Suplyd, and what do you still buy direct? Why the split?"
2. "When did you last check Suplyd's price against your old supplier?"
3. "Who decides which supplier gets an order, and how do they decide?"
4. "Do your branches order on their own, or does someone approve?"

## Objections
| They say | Answer (one line) | Proof / next step |
|---|---|---|
| "Suplyd already gives us good prices." | "Maybe. Let's check: send me last week's Suplyd order and two other quotes for the same items." | Run a comparison on their real basket in the demo |
| "Suplyd is one app. Why add another?" | "Suplyd is where you buy. We're where you decide. The pilot is 14 days on your top 10–20 items." | Pilot scope |
| "Suplyd delivers. Do you?" | "No. Your suppliers, Suplyd included, still deliver. We make sure each order goes to the right one." | — |
| "Suplyd works with Foodics." | "We don't integrate with Foodics yet; that's being tracked. The pilot doesn't need the POS." | Fit gaps, `first-customer-gtm-plan.md` |

## When to walk away
- Single-site restaurant with no purchasing role: Suplyd's convenience wins, and our approvals and branch model add nothing.
- The buyer gets *everything* from Suplyd and has no other suppliers to compare.
- The pain is delivery reliability, not price.

## Do not say
"Suplyd is more expensive" (unverified: prove it on their basket) · anything about Suplyd's margins or business model as fact.
