# R2.5 Reporting and Hardening Product Critique

Date: 2026-09-14

Author: Agy product critique (delegated), reviewed by the orchestrator

## Summary Verdict

R2.5 addresses essential roadmap commitments (scheduled reports, weekly digests, hardened exports, performance, and security review), but the current specification is blocked by critical gaps in Arabic PDF rendering, missing locale tracking in schemas, UUID-only export layouts, and passive digest/reporting surfaces that omit unverified savings backlogs. Furthermore, branch authorization is absent from artifact retrieval endpoints, creating severe cross-branch financial leakage risks. These five blockers and six major findings must be resolved in specs and tasks before Wave 16 implementation commences.

## Numbered Findings

### 1. Severity: blocker | Question 4: Arabic/RTL reporting and printable layouts

**Finding**: ReportLab PDF architecture completely omits Arabic text reshaping and bidirectional reordering, guaranteeing corrupted, disconnected, and reversed Arabic output.

**Evidence**: `specs/012-reporting-hardening/research.md` R8 (lines 108–120); `specs/012-reporting-hardening/spec.md` FR-015 (lines 227–229); `specs/012-reporting-hardening/tasks.md` T015 (lines 104–109); `.specify/memory/constitution.md` Principle VII (lines 156–169).

**Critique**: Research R8 states that "Arabic PDFs embed an Arabic-capable open-license font (Noto Naskh Arabic or equivalent OFL font...) and draw right-to-left layout; the current Helvetica usage remains only for Latin locales." This plan reveals a fatal misconception about ReportLab in Python. Embedding a TrueType font supplies glyph shapes but does not perform Arabic text shaping or Unicode Bidirectional (BiDi) reordering. In Arabic script, characters are cursive and alter their visual presentation (isolated, initial, medial, final) depending on adjacent characters. Without passing strings through an Arabic reshaping engine (such as `arabic-reshaper`) and a BiDi layout engine (such as `python-bidi`), ReportLab draws characters in their isolated form and in reverse (left-to-right). Moreover, mixed alphanumeric phrases (e.g., Arabic product names alongside Latin SKUs, invoice dates, and currency notations like "500 SAR") will have their numeric and Latin segments inverted. Simply drawing text at right-aligned coordinates with a Noto font will produce illegible gibberish, violating Principle VII ("Language Correct from the Schema Up").

**Recommendation**: Add `arabic-reshaper` and `python-bidi` (or `uharfbuzz`) to API backend dependencies in `apps/api/pyproject.toml`. Update research R8, FR-015, and task T015 to define an explicit PDF text preparation pipeline (`raw_text -> i18n_lookup -> arabic_reshaper.reshape() -> bidi.algorithm.get_display() -> canvas.drawString()`). Require an automated test in T019 asserting correctly connected Arabic ligatures and order.

### 2. Severity: blocker | Question 4: Arabic/RTL reporting and printable layouts

**Finding**: Missing `locale` attribute on `report_schedule`, `ExportCreate`, and `export_job` entities forces background workers to guess rendering languages and prevents users from identifying artifact language.

**Evidence**: `specs/012-reporting-hardening/data-model.md` lines 9–29, 78–95; `specs/012-reporting-hardening/spec.md` FR-001, FR-015; `apps/api/src/procurepilot_api/modules/exports/schemas.py` lines 25–31.

**Critique**: FR-015 mandates rendering report labels from `packages/i18n` in English or Arabic. However, neither `report_schedule` nor `export_job` nor `ExportCreate` includes a `locale` field. Scheduled generation executes in background workers (`report_scheduler` / `export_worker`) without an incoming HTTP request, without an `Accept-Language` header, and without a user session JWT. Without an explicit `locale` column on `report_schedule`, the worker must either hardcode English or guess from tenant metadata. In bilingual organizations across the GCC, a buyer may operate in English while the business owner requires Arabic PDF exports. If `report_schedule` cannot store the target report language, bilingual workflows break. Furthermore, because `export_job` does not persist the generation locale, members viewing the Reports center cannot determine whether a generated artifact is in English or Arabic prior to downloading it.

**Recommendation**: Amend `data-model.md`, migrations (`20260915000002_report_schedule.sql`, `20260915000004_export_job_reporting_extensions.sql`), and schemas (`ExportCreate`, `ExportJob`, `ReportSchedule`) to include `locale: Locale` (`en` | `ar`, defaulting to member preference or tenant default). Update FR-001, FR-004, FR-015, T002, T004, T011, T014, and T021.

### 3. Severity: blocker | Question 2: Operational usefulness of exports after printing and sharing

**Finding**: Existing export renderers emit internal database UUIDs and raw REST API paths, omitting critical operational business identifiers (supplier name, product name, PO reference, and human verification links).

**Evidence**: `apps/api/src/procurepilot_api/modules/exports/renderers.py` lines 6–17, 58–72, 78–93; `specs/012-reporting-hardening/spec.md` FR-002, FR-015, FR-016; `docs/user/user-documentation.md` §15 lines 514, 526.

**Critique**: The existing export implementation in `renderers.py` (which R2.5 builds upon in T015) emits raw database keys: `Saving ID` (UUID), `Supplier ID` (UUID), and an evidence reference formatted as `f"/api/v1/savings/{row['id']}/evidence"`. It completely omits Supplier Name, Product Name/SKU, Branch Name, Normalized Quantity & Unit, and Purchase Order / Reference ID. Furthermore, in the PDF renderer, rows are drawn as crude truncated text strings (`line[:115]`) without structured table borders, header blocks, or summary totals. If a branch manager prints this PDF and hands it to a business owner, or an accountant imports the CSV into an accounting package:
1. The supplier is represented as an unidentifiable 36-character hexadecimal string (`str(row["supplier_id"])`).
2. The product purchased is unnamed.
3. The evidence reference is an internal backend REST endpoint requiring a Bearer JWT, which cannot be resolved on paper or clicked in external software.
The exported documents fail the fundamental requirement of an "audit-ready report of verified savings" documented in user documentation §16.

**Recommendation**: Update FR-002, FR-015, FR-016, and T015 to define a comprehensive business schema for savings exports: `Saving ID` (short reference), `Recorded Date`, `Branch Name`, `Supplier Name`, `Product Name`, `SKU / Unit`, `Quantity Ordered`, `Baseline Unit Price`, `Baseline Total Amount & Currency`, `Actual Unit Price`, `Actual Total Paid & Currency`, `Verified Saving Amount & Currency`, `PO / Reference ID`, and `Evidence Web URL` (`https://<domain>/savings/{id}/evidence`). The PDF renderer must utilize ReportLab `Table` layouts with formal corporate headers and page numbering.

### 4. Severity: blocker | Questions 1 and 3: Actionable digests vs. passive dashboards (Principle IV)

**Finding**: Weekly digests report historical, locked verified savings while omitting pending verification backlogs and pending approvals, violating Principle IV ("Every Insight Ends in an Action").

**Evidence**: `specs/012-reporting-hardening/spec.md` US2 (lines 48–52), FR-009 (lines 213–215); `docs/user/user-documentation.md` §15 (lines 495, 523); `.specify/memory/constitution.md` Principle IV (lines 109–124).

**Critique**: FR-009 defines weekly digest contents as: (1) verified savings recorded in the period, (2) new anomalies detected, and (3) offers expiring within 7 days. Verified savings are immutable and closed (user docs §15: "once verified, they cannot be edited or deleted"). Presenting only verified savings displays backward-looking history where no further human action is possible. Meanwhile, the actual operational bottlenecks in ProcurePilot's savings cycle—**pending purchase outcomes awaiting verification** by an owner/buyer, and **purchase requests awaiting approval** (introduced in R2.1)—are completely excluded from the digest. If an owner sees "Verified savings this week: $0" simply because three purchases sit in "Pending Verification" status, the digest delivers misleading feedback and fails to prompt the human intervention needed to prove value.

**Recommendation**: Amend FR-009, `data-model.md` (`DigestView`), and T023/T027 to require two high-priority actionable sections in the weekly digest:
1. **Pending Verifications**: Count and potential savings amount of unverified outcomes, with a deep link to `/savings?status=pending` and a clear call-to-action ("Verify 3 outcomes to lock in SAR 4,200 savings").
2. **Pending Approvals**: Count and monetary value of purchase requests awaiting the subscriber's approval, deep-linking to `/approvals`.
Map to FR-009, T023, T024, and T027.

### 5. Severity: blocker | Questions 2 and 5: Branch authorization and data isolation gaps

**Finding**: Reports center listing and artifact download endpoints lack branch-level authorization checks, exposing tenant-wide financial summaries and other branches' spend data to branch-scoped users.

**Evidence**: `specs/012-reporting-hardening/spec.md` FR-004, FR-005, Edge Cases (line 184); `specs/012-reporting-hardening/data-model.md` lines 39, 98; `supabase/migrations/20260821000034_value_proof_rls.sql` lines 18–26; `docs/user/user-documentation.md` §17 (line 566).

**Critique**: Phase 2 introduced branch segregation (R2.0, R2.1). In `spec.md` Edge Cases, the specification notes: "If a report or digest is requested for a branch the caller cannot see, the request must resolve as not found or be refused with a validation error." However, `export_job` RLS in `value_proof_rls.sql` is tenant-wide (`tenant_id = current_tenant_id()`), allowing any authenticated tenant member to select all rows. When FR-004 exposes `GET /api/v1/reports/artifacts` and FR-005 exposes `GET /api/v1/exports/{id}/download`, neither endpoint is specified to validate branch authorization against the requesting member's role. A branch manager or restricted buyer could call `GET /reports/artifacts`, enumerate executive spend reports across all branches, and download full-company financial data via signed URLs. This creates a severe data-leakage vulnerability across branch boundaries within the same tenant.

**Recommendation**: Explicitly specify branch authorization in FR-004, FR-005, FR-018, and T012/T013:
1. `GET /reports/artifacts` must filter artifact listings by the caller's authorized branches; artifacts containing company-wide or unauthorized branch filters must be excluded from the listing for branch-scoped roles.
2. `GET /exports/{id}/download` must verify that the requesting member has access to the branch associated with the artifact's `filters->>'branch_id'`; unauthorized requests must return 404 Not Found (never 403 Forbidden, per non-negotiables).
3. Add cross-branch artifact isolation test cases to T008 and T019.

### 6. Severity: major | Question 2: Undefined schema and multi-currency aggregation in new export kinds

**Finding**: `spend_by_supplier` and `alerts_summary` lack schema definitions, column layouts, and multi-currency grouping rules, risking corrupted sums under Principle VII.

**Evidence**: `specs/012-reporting-hardening/spec.md` FR-002; `specs/012-reporting-hardening/data-model.md` lines 18, 109–112; `.specify/memory/constitution.md` Principle VII (lines 156–169).

**Critique**: FR-002 introduces `spend_by_supplier` and `alerts_summary` as supported export kinds across CSV and XLSX. However, neither `spec.md` nor `data-model.md` defines their field specifications. Under Principle VII, "Monetary values MUST store an explicit currency; no implicit single-currency assumptions." If a supplier invoices in multiple currencies (e.g. SAR and USD, or EUR and USD), a spend aggregation query cannot sum amounts across differing currencies into a single figure without converting via verified FX rates (which Phase 2 does not support). If `reporting_purchases_for_period` aggregates spend naively, the resulting spreadsheet will report corrupted financial totals. Additionally, `alerts_summary` export columns are completely undefined.

**Recommendation**: Amend `spec.md` FR-002 and `data-model.md` to specify exact columns and aggregation constraints:
1. `spend_by_supplier`: Must aggregate and group by `(supplier_id, supplier_name, currency)`. Columns: `Supplier Name`, `Tax/VAT Number`, `Currency`, `Total Spend Invoiced`, `Order Count`, `Verified Savings Realized`, and `Primary Branch`.
2. `alerts_summary`: Columns: `Alert ID`, `Triggered Date`, `Alert Type`, `Severity`, `Supplier Name`, `Product Name`, `Branch Name`, `Estimated Financial Exposure & Currency`, `Status` (Active/Dismissed), `Dismissed By`, and `Dismissal Reason`.
Map to FR-002, T005, T014, and T015.

### 7. Severity: major | Questions 1 and 3: Reports Center risks becoming a passive administrative archive

**Finding**: The Reports center lists historical file metadata and download buttons without embedding procurement actions or insight indicators, skirting Principle IV.

**Evidence**: `specs/012-reporting-hardening/spec.md` FR-004; `specs/012-reporting-hardening/plan.md` lines 44–45; `specs/012-reporting-hardening/tasks.md` T020; `.specify/memory/constitution.md` Principle IV.

**Critique**: Plan.md lines 44–45 asserts constitutional compliance by stating: "no passive dashboard — the Reports center carries download and schedule actions." However, downloading an artifact and pausing a schedule are administrative file-management operations, not procurement decisions. When an owner views a completed `alerts_summary` showing 15 anomalies, or a `spend_by_supplier` report showing 85% spend concentration with a single supplier, the table row only shows `row_count: 15` and a `Download` button. The user must download a spreadsheet, open it in an external application, inspect it, and manually navigate back into ProcurePilot to act.

**Recommendation**: Expand FR-004 and T020 so each artifact row in the Reports center displays:
1. A summary highlight pill (e.g., "Total Verified Savings: SAR 18,500" or "Active Anomalies: 7").
2. Contextual operational deep links alongside the download action:
   - For `alerts_summary`: "Investigate in Alerts Inbox" (pre-filtered by period).
   - For `savings_ledger`: "Review Ledger & Pending Verifications".
   - For `spend_by_supplier`: "Benchmark Pricing in Smart Compare".
Map to FR-004 and T020.

### 8. Severity: major | Questions 4 and 5: Digest email lacks RTL layout and standard email headers

**Finding**: The digest email specification omits HTML RTL layout attributes, system font fallbacks, and standard unsubscribe/frequency management headers.

**Evidence**: `specs/012-reporting-hardening/spec.md` FR-010, FR-028; `specs/012-reporting-hardening/research.md` R7; `specs/012-reporting-hardening/tasks.md` T024.

**Critique**: FR-010 and T024 specify email delivery via SMTP. While web screens use CSS logical properties, HTML emails operate under strict client rendering constraints (e.g. desktop Outlook, Gmail mobile, Apple Mail). If an email is sent in Arabic without explicit `<html dir="rtl" lang="ar">`, inline `text-align: right`, and email-safe Arabic fonts (e.g., Tahoma, Arial, Segoe UI), email clients will display RTL text misaligned, bullets reversed, and deep link action buttons positioned awkwardly. Furthermore, neither `spec.md` nor `tasks.md` defines standard email headers (`List-Unsubscribe`) or footer links to manage preferences. Commercial mail servers frequently penalize automated weekly digests lacking direct unsubscribe mechanisms.

**Recommendation**: Update FR-010 and T024 to require:
1. Multipart email rendering (`text/html` and `text/plain`).
2. HTML email templates with dynamic `dir="rtl"` / `dir="ltr"` and inline CSS styling verified across major clients.
3. Digest email footer containing a direct link to manage subscription settings (`/reports/digest-settings`) and standard `List-Unsubscribe` headers.
Map to FR-010, FR-028, and T024.

### 9. Severity: major | Question 4: CSV exports lack UTF-8 BOM, producing corrupted Arabic text in Excel

**Finding**: CSV export rendering does not include a UTF-8 Byte Order Mark (BOM), causing Arabic characters to display as garbled mojibake in Microsoft Excel.

**Evidence**: `specs/012-reporting-hardening/spec.md` FR-012, FR-015; `specs/012-reporting-hardening/research.md` R2, R8; `specs/012-reporting-hardening/tasks.md` T015.

**Critique**: FR-012 expands CSV export across all report kinds, and FR-015 requires Arabic headers. In corporate environments across the Middle East, CSV files are opened directly in desktop Microsoft Excel on Windows. By default, Excel does not detect UTF-8 encoding in CSV files unless the byte stream starts with the UTF-8 Byte Order Mark (`\xef\xbb\xbf`). Without the BOM, Excel decodes the file using ANSI (Windows-1252 or Windows-1256), turning Arabic supplier names, product descriptions, and column titles into incomprehensible mojibake (e.g. `Ø§Ù„Ù…ÙˆØ±Ø¯`).

**Recommendation**: Amend T015 and `renderers.py` to ensure the CSV renderer prepends `codecs.BOM_UTF8` to the byte output for all CSV exports. Add a test asserting BOM presence in `test_export_download.py`. Map to FR-012, FR-015, and T015.

### 10. Severity: major | Question 5: Missing digest onboarding threatens Phase 2 retention targets (Gate G2)

**Finding**: Digest subscriptions are designed as an unprompted, obscure configuration screen without onboarding defaults or automated enrollment, jeopardizing Phase 2 retention targets.

**Evidence**: `specs/012-reporting-hardening/spec.md` US2, FR-008; `docs/roadmap/procurepilot_roadmap.md` §2.3 Gate G2 (line 216); `specs/012-reporting-hardening/tasks.md` T027.

**Critique**: Roadmap §2.3 specifies Gate G2 exit criteria: "Workflow-origination ≥70%; mobile adoption target met; retention ≥90%." Spec.md US2 (line 54) identifies the digest as "the retention surface that pulls members back to the workflow between sessions." However, the current plan treats digests as an entirely passive opt-in feature hidden inside `apps/web/src/app/features/reports/digest-settings/` (T027). If users are not prompted or defaulted into a weekly digest upon joining a workspace, uptake will remain negligible (<5%), neutralizing the digest's capability to drive the 90% retention required to pass Gate G2.

**Recommendation**: Update FR-008 and T023/T027:
1. Automatically provision an active `weekly_digest` subscription for new members upon invitation acceptance (defaulting to email channel if SMTP is enabled, otherwise in-app).
2. Display a dismissible onboarding prompt on the main dashboard for existing users highlighting the weekly digest.
3. Ensure simple one-click pause/resume toggles are accessible in both the digest settings and user profile.
Map to FR-008, T003, T023, and T027.

### 11. Severity: major | User clarity and weekly value hierarchy

**Finding**: The planned weekly digest structure risks burying core verified savings metrics below operational alert lists and expiring quotations.

**Evidence**: `specs/012-reporting-hardening/spec.md` US2 (lines 48–52), FR-009 (lines 213–215); `specs/012-reporting-hardening/data-model.md` lines 138–143.

**Critique**: ProcurePilot's core brand promise is proving money saved. In high-volume food service and retail environments, dozens of quotations may expire each week and multiple anomaly alerts may trigger. If the digest renders expiring offers or alerts at the top of the email, or if it lists voluminous line items before the financial total, business owners viewing the digest on mobile devices will see a wall of alerts and fail to see the savings ProcurePilot generated for them that week.

**Recommendation**: Standardize the information architecture of `DigestView` and the email template in FR-009, `data-model.md`, and T024/T027:
- **Hero Header (Above the fold)**: Prominent KPI card displaying:
  1. Verified Savings Realized This Week (large font, explicit currency).
  2. Potential Savings in Pending Outcomes (with "Verify Now" button).
- **Middle Section (Risk Interceptions)**: Top 3 Critical Anomalies (with "Review in Inbox" link).
- **Bottom Section (Expiring Quotations)**: Top 5 Expiring Offers ranked by purchase volume (with "Compare Now" link).
Map to FR-009, T023, T024, and T027.

### 12. Severity: minor | Question 2: Empty states in exports lack formal audit header blocks

**Finding**: Empty export files render as bare unformatted text without report metadata, appearing indistinguishable from corrupted files or generation failures.

**Evidence**: `apps/api/src/procurepilot_api/modules/exports/renderers.py` lines 32–33, 55–56; `specs/012-reporting-hardening/spec.md` Edge Cases (line 166).

**Critique**: When an export yields zero rows, the existing renderer outputs a single plain sentence: `"No verified savings matched the requested period."` In a formal financial audit or tax review, an exported spreadsheet or PDF lacking company name, generation timestamp, filter criteria, and column headers looks like an aborted database query rather than a verified statement of zero spend.

**Recommendation**: Update FR-015 and T015 to require that empty exports render complete report header metadata (Workspace Name, Report Kind, Filter Summary, Period Range, Generation Timestamp, and Rule Version) alongside standard table headers and a formal empty status: "Zero records found matching the applied criteria." Map to FR-015 and T015.

### 13. Severity: minor | Question 5: Disconnected user navigation between Savings Ledger and Reports Center

**Finding**: The user flow between `/savings/export` and the new `/reports` center is fragmented, leaving users confused about where past exports reside and how to schedule recurring exports.

**Evidence**: `docs/user/user-documentation.md` §15, §16; `specs/012-reporting-hardening/spec.md` US1, FR-004; `specs/012-reporting-hardening/tasks.md` T020, T021.

**Critique**: Currently, users export savings from `/savings` by clicking "Export Savings", which navigates to `/savings/export`. R2.5 introduces a separate Reports center at `/reports` (FR-004, T020). However, the plan retains `/savings/export` as a standalone screen (T021) without clear navigational cross-links to `/reports`. A user generating an export on `/savings/export` who navigates away has no indication that their completed file is preserved in the Reports center. Similarly, a user configuring filters on `/savings/export` has no shortcut to convert those filters into a weekly recurring schedule.

**Recommendation**: In T020 and T021:
1. Add a direct link on `/savings/export` and `/savings`: "View Past Export History & Scheduled Reports" pointing to `/reports`.
2. On `/savings/export`, add a secondary action button: "Schedule as Weekly Report", pre-populating the schedule form at `/reports/schedules/new` with the selected filters.
3. Update user documentation in T040.
Map to FR-001, FR-004, T020, T021, and T040.

### 14. Severity: minor | Operational integrity and schedule ownership lifecycle

**Finding**: The schedule lifecycle does not define behavior when the creator's membership is deactivated, risking orphaned or unexpectedly silenced executive reports.

**Evidence**: `specs/012-reporting-hardening/spec.md` Edge Cases (lines 176–177); `specs/012-reporting-hardening/data-model.md` lines 27, 65; `specs/012-reporting-hardening/tasks.md` T016.

**Critique**: `data-model.md` establishes that digest subscriptions skip deactivated members. However, for `report_schedule`, the table carries `created_by_membership_id`, but artifacts are workspace-scoped and consumed by multiple stakeholders (e.g. weekly board packs). If a buyer who configured a weekly savings schedule leaves the company and their membership is suspended, the specification is silent on whether the schedule halts, fails, or continues running.

**Recommendation**: Clarify in `spec.md` Edge Cases and `schedules.py` (T011, T016) that `report_schedule` is an organizational asset. If the creating membership is deactivated, active schedules continue to execute under tenant authority with `created_by_membership_id` preserved for provenance. Any tenant owner or buyer may edit, pause, or reassign the schedule. Map to FR-001, T011, and T016.

## Recommendation for Wave 16

The findings above are partitioned into items that must be resolved in specifications and database migrations before Wave 16 code generation begins, versus items that can be addressed during feature implementation.

### Must Fold into Spec, Data Model, and Tasks Before Wave 16 Implementation

1. **Arabic PDF Shaping & BiDi (Finding 1)**: Add `arabic-reshaper` and `python-bidi` to dependencies, update research R8, FR-015, and task T015 to specify the reshaping pipeline before writing renderers.
2. **Schema `locale` Tracking (Finding 2)**: Add `locale` column to `report_schedule` (T002 migration), `export_job` (T004 migration), and API schemas (`ExportCreate`, `ExportJob`, `ReportSchedule`), ensuring workers and users know the artifact language.
3. **Export Schema Hardening & De-anonymization (Finding 3 & Finding 6)**: Define human-readable business columns (Product Name, Supplier Name, Branch Name, PO Reference, human web URLs) and multi-currency grouping rules for all three export kinds in FR-002, FR-015, FR-016, and T015.
4. **Actionable Weekly Digest Scope (Finding 4 & Finding 11)**: Expand FR-009, `DigestView`, and T023 to place verified savings at the top, and add actionable sections for pending outcome verifications and pending approvals.
5. **Branch Authorization on Artifacts (Finding 5)**: Update FR-004, FR-005, and tasks T008, T012, T013 to enforce branch visibility on `GET /reports/artifacts` and `GET /exports/{id}/download`.
6. **UTF-8 BOM for CSV (Finding 9)**: Amend task T015 to mandate UTF-8 BOM encoding for Excel compatibility across all CSV outputs.

### May Ride Along During Feature Implementation (Wave 17–18)

1. **Reports Center Insight Badges (Finding 7)**: Implement operational deep-link buttons alongside download actions during Reports center UI construction (T020).
2. **Email HTML RTL & Unsubscribe Headers (Finding 8)**: Implement during digest worker and email renderer construction (T024).
3. **Digest Onboarding & Default Provisioning (Finding 10)**: Implement during invitation acceptance and digest service wiring (T023, T027).
4. **Export Empty State Audit Header (Finding 12)**: Implement in export renderer error/empty handling (T015).
5. **Savings Ledger to Reports Center Cross-Links (Finding 13)**: Add UI navigation links during frontend tasks T020 and T021.
6. **Schedule Ownership Lifecycle on Creator Deactivation (Finding 14)**: Add edge-case handling in scheduler claim queries during task T016.
