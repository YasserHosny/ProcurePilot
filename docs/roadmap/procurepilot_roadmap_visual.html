02 — VISION & THESIS

Why the product exists

Large organisations employ procurement teams. Small businesses buy the same categories, repeatedly, with none of that infrastructure — relying on habit, relationships, and memory.

Vision, mission, positioning

Locked

M

Mission. Turn fragmented supplier information into trusted, comparable, actionable purchasing decisions — and prove the money saved.

P

Positioning. An AI purchasing co-pilot and lightweight procurement operating system for small businesses.

N

10 — DATA & QUALITY

Trust is the product

A wrong match produces a fabricated saving — worse than no saving at all. Data quality is therefore a permanent workstream, not a phase.

Expected data-quality maturity

Match accuracy vs. field completeness

Months after a tenant onboards, not programme months. Month-1 accuracy of 72% means roughly 1 in 4 comparisons is wrong. This is precisely why Phase 0 value must come from human-verified concierge output, not autonomous AI.

Cold-start tension

Key risk
Pricing promises month-1 ROI. Accuracy assumptions say 72% match / 65% completeness in month 1. These cannot both be true autonomously.
Resolution. Month-1 value comes from concierge output with human verification. The review / exception queue is therefore a first-class product surface in the MVP — arguably more important than the executive dashboard.

Architectural principles

8 rules
1

Modular monolith first. Extract the extraction worker and optimiser only when scale demands it.

2

Normalisation is pure, versioned, replayable. Store inputs plus rule version.

3

Confidence and provenance are columns, not metadata blobs.

4

Bitemporal price data. Defensible savings requires "what did we know, and when?"

5

Outcomes are event-sourced. Mutable rows destroy the audit trail.

6

Canonical spine + tenant overlay. Enables future benchmarking without data leakage.

7

Human-in-the-loop is architectural. Review queue has its own state machine and metrics.

8

Multi-currency, multi-tax, bilingual from the schema up. Retrofitting is disproportionately expensive.
