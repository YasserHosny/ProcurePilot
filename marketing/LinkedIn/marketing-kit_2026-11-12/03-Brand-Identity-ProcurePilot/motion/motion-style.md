# Motion graphics style — binding direction for every ProcurePilot video

Adopted 2026-10-22 by the owner. It applies to the demo video (PP-6) and any later video or
animated asset. Part A is the owner's direction, kept as given. Part B shows how it applies to
ProcurePilot. Part C lists where the existing cuts fall short of it.

## A. The owner's direction (2026-10-22)

**Role.** Act as a senior motion graphics designer and creative director. Apply these principles
to the project's existing video, script, content and context. Treat them as a flexible style
system, not a fixed template.

1. **Visual style and art direction:** contemporary, editorial-inspired motion graphics.
   - Bold kinetic typography with purposeful movement.
   - Layered compositions of type, photography, graphic shapes and visual elements.
   - Clean but rich layouts with strong hierarchy and intentional negative space.
   - Asymmetry, geometric framing and controlled contrast.
   - Cutouts, overlapping layers, subtle paper-like textures and dimensional shadows where they fit.
   - Minimalism balanced against visual complexity.
   - Dynamic transitions that connect scenes.
   - It must feel custom-designed, not like a template.
2. **Adaptive colour system:** no fixed palette.
   - Analyse the existing visuals and brand identity first.
   - Use official primary and secondary colours when they exist; otherwise build a palette that
     fits the concept, industry, tone and audience.
   - Set a clear hierarchy: dominant, supporting, neutral background, accent.
   - Use contrast to guide attention and create rhythm, and apply colour consistently across type,
     shapes, backgrounds and transitions.
   - Keep text readable, and don't introduce colours that conflict with the brand.
3. **Brand identity integration:** weave the brand in rather than overlaying it.
   - Use the official colours consistently; supporting colours are fine when brand-compatible.
   - Never redesign or distort the logo; respect its proportions and clear space.
   - Use the approved typography.
   - Use brand elements in transitions, compositions, backgrounds and accents.
   - Translate the brand's personality into motion.
   - Keep scenes consistent without making them identical.
   - Official assets win any conflict.
   - Never invent brand rules.
4. **Typography and kinetic text:** type is a core visual element, not a caption.
   - Use scale, weight, alignment and spacing for hierarchy.
   - Reveal words by masking, cropping, scaling, directional movement and staggered entrances.
   - Pair large display type with small supporting text.
   - Use type as a transition, a shape or an anchor.
   - Keep it legible throughout.
   - Respect writing direction: for Arabic, correct RTL, connected letterforms and natural reading order.
   - Never invent, distort, misspell or rewrite the project's actual text.
5. **Motion language:**
   - Fast acceleration into controlled deceleration.
   - Directional moves with subtle motion blur.
   - Smooth ease-outs and crisp final positions.
   - Staggered timing.
   - Restrained overshoot and secondary motion.
   - Layered parallax and shallow 2.5D depth.
   - Mask reveals and shape transformations.
   - Vary the motion; don't apply one preset everywhere. Motion carries meaning, hierarchy,
     momentum and attention.
6. **Composition, layers and depth:** type with shapes; photography and cutouts; foreground,
   middle and background layers; overlaps with subtle physical shadows; geometric frames, lines
   and panels; restrained parallax. Every layer has a purpose, and every frame stays readable.
7. **Transitions and continuity:** transitions tell the story. Options include type wipes and
   text masks, shape wipes and expanding colour planes, cutout reveals, camera and parallax moves,
   elements carried from one scene into the next, and re-composition by scaling, cropping and
   directional reveals. Keep one motion logic and rhythm across the video.
8. **Texture, imagery and treatments (optional):** grain, halftone, monochrome photography,
   cutouts, gradients, geometric illustration, editorial framing, shadows and layered textures.
   Use them only where they strengthen the identity.
9. **Pacing and rhythm:** fast impact on key moments, and enough hold for messages to register.
   Alternate dense and restrained moments, and keep the content's natural flow.
10. **Avoid:** generic tech aesthetics (neon cyberpunk, glowing UI, holographic dashboards,
    stereotypical AI imagery, generic SaaS animation, needless 3D) unless they truly fit. Also
    avoid excessive transitions, random movement, clutter, inconsistent colours, and effects that
    distract from the message.
11. **Execution rules:**
    - Work on the existing video and content. No new script, storyboard, shot map or prompt collection.
    - Don't change the message, the facts or the intended content.
    - Don't impose a predefined palette.
    - Use the brand when it is available.
    - Keep the motion language consistent while varying the scenes.
    - Decide from the actual project. Clarity, craft, brand alignment, purposeful motion and
      cohesion come first.

**Goal:** a premium, expressive, editorial-inspired motion graphics experience, recognisable by
its composition, type, layering and motion rather than by a fixed palette.
