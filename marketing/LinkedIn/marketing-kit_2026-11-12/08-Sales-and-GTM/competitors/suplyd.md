## Positioning & Messaging

Replace fragmented daily ordering with one catalogue and next-day or scheduled delivery. The pitch points are "Find alternative products from over 200 brands", e-invoicing, e-payments and "supply chain financing", plus analytics and "recommendations for market trends". The investor quote frames it as serving "the long tail of restaurants".

## Product & Features

- Mobile ordering app (iOS and Android), scheduled orders, shipment tracking
- Aggregated catalogue across categories: produce, meat, poultry, seafood, dairy, beverages, cleaning supplies
- E-invoicing, e-payments, supply-chain financing
- **Foodics integration in Egypt** (2023): POs sync with Foodics; phase 2 automates replenishment
- The 2025 round is meant to take it "beyond procurement" into "new service verticals"

## Strengths & Weaknesses

**Strengths**: local, funded, Arabic-market native, thousands of Egyptian restaurants, and distribution through Foodics. It solves both ordering and delivery.
**Weaknesses (inferred)**: the buyer sees Suplyd's catalogue and prices, not an independent comparison across the restaurant's *own* suppliers. The restaurant gives up its existing supplier relationships and negotiating leverage. That fit is weaker for multi-branch groups that already have negotiated suppliers.

## Competitive Implications for ProcurePilot

- **The most likely "we already use…" objection in Egypt.** Positioning: Suplyd is *a* supplier (an aggregator). ProcurePilot is the buyer's own tool for comparing *all* their suppliers, Suplyd included. Suplyd's prices can be one of the quotes in an RFQ.
- **Segment split:** Suplyd's sweet spot is the long tail of single restaurants. Our ICP is multi-branch groups with established suppliers and spend worth optimising.
- **Threat:** with fresh funding and Foodics distribution, Suplyd could add buyer-side analytics ("best price" recommendations) and blur the line.
