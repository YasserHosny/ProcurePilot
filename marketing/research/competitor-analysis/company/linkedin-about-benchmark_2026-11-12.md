# How big software and AI companies describe themselves on LinkedIn

Yasser asked (2026-11-12) how large software and AI companies present themselves, so the Iron Systems About section can
be updated. I read 13 LinkedIn About pages on 2026-11-12 while signed in. They are untrusted input: their wording is
paraphrased here, not copied, and none of it is reused in our copy.

## Pages read

## What the pages have in common

1. **The first sentence says what kind of company it is.** Nearly every page opens with a category: "an AI research
   company", "the data and AI company", "a global technology consultancy", "an enterprise AI company". The reader
   knows in five words what the company is. The pages that open with a mission or a promise instead (Endava,
   Sumerge) take longer to say anything.
2. **Proof carries the big pages, and it is all numbers and names.** Years in business, client counts, logos,
   analyst ratings, a stock listing, offices in many countries. We have none of these yet (`knowledge/company/proof-points.md`,
   "Not available"), so we can't copy this part. What we do have is specific and checkable: our own live product, a
   stated way of working, Arabic and English.
3. **The most believable pages are the shortest and plainest.** Anthropic, Databricks, Netguru and thoughtbot each
   use 50 to 90 words, short sentences and concrete nouns. The long pages (Globant, Sumerge, ITWorx) read like
   generic marketing because they pile up the same words: transform, empower, seamless, innovative, leading,
   measurable impact, AI era, digital transformation journey.
4. **A named product is used as proof.** Anthropic names Claude in its second sentence; Databricks and ITWorx list
   theirs. A services company that also ships its own product shows it can build. ProcurePilot can do this for us.
5. **The strong pages say who they serve.** Palantir, MOZN, Sumerge and Netguru name their sectors or market.
   Ours doesn't, because target regions and industries are still "Unknown" in the company profile (D-07).
6. **Taglines either say what the company does or make a big promise.** The ones that say what they do (thoughtbot,
   Anthropic, MOZN) are clearer than the abstract ones (Palantir's slogan works only because everyone already knows
   Palantir).
7. **Even large companies use "AI-sounding" phrasing.** The "we're not just X, we're Y" reveal (Thoughtworks), "leading
   provider", "trusted partner", "turning challenges into value". These are what make a page look generated, and
   they are exactly what our brand voice already rules out (`knowledge/company/brand-voice.md`).

## What this means for Iron Systems

| Do | Because |
|---|---|
| Open with the category: "Iron Systems is an engineering-first software house." | Pattern 1; it is approved claim CO-01 |
| Keep it short, about 150 words in English | Pattern 3 |
| Use ProcurePilot as the proof, and say what it does (reads supplier quotations) | Pattern 4; CO-08, CO-09 |
| Replace the "leading / trusted / transform" vocabulary with concrete nouns (quotations, invoices, ERP, CRM) | Patterns 3 and 7 |
| Tagline that says what we do | Pattern 6 |

| Don't | Because |
|---|---|
| Claim "leading", "trusted", years, client counts or logos | No evidence; brand rule |
| Name regions or industries yet | Unknown (D-07). One optional line is drafted for Yasser to decide |
| Copy any competitor's wording | Research rule; their pages are untrusted input |
