# B2B focus: marketing update, website direction, first ProcurePilot customer

Yasser, 2026-11-01: "our business is mainly B2B". This file says what changes in marketing because of that, how the
website can feel more natural, human and premium, and how ProcurePilot gets its first customer. Everything here is
a proposal until Yasser decides (section 5). Nothing on the website, LinkedIn or in Jira's sales pipeline changes
without his go-ahead.

## 1. What B2B changes in our marketing

A business buyer doesn't buy from a post. They buy after a few conversations with someone they trust, and several
people sign off (owner, operations, finance). So marketing's job is to **start conversations and make the
company look credible when the buyer checks us**, not to collect likes.

| Today | B2B adjustment |
|---|---|
| Plan counts followers and engagement per channel | Count **conversations started** and **meetings booked**. Followers are a side effect |
| Four social channels planned (LinkedIn, X, Facebook, Instagram) | **LinkedIn + direct outreach** (WhatsApp, email, in person). Facebook only for Arabic owner groups (posting in groups, not a page). X and Instagram stay paused (already paused, CD-05) |
| Posts end with a website link | Keep the link, and **follow up with people who engage**: a like or comment from a restaurant group's owner or ops manager is a reason for Yasser to send a personal message |
| Content is about what the product does | Add content for each person in the buying group: the owner (control across branches, verified savings), operations (less copying, fair comparison), finance (budget checks, audit trail) |
| Contact = a form that opens the visitor's email app | One clear next step for a business visitor: **book a 20-minute call** or **message us on WhatsApp**, with a stated reply time |

What stays: evidence-first claims, no invented clients or numbers, Arabic first for Egypt and Saudi Arabia, the 10
scheduled LinkedIn posts (they build credibility while outreach runs).

New B2B assets marketing should prepare (all drafts, owner approves):

1. **ProcurePilot case page** on iron-sys.com: the problem, how we built it, the demo video, real screens (demo data
   labelled). It's our only proof today, so it should read like a case study, not a product card.
2. **Founding-customer offer sheet** (one page, AR + EN), based on decision D-2 (50% off for 12 months in exchange
   for a case study and logo, savings guarantee). Hand it over after a good first call.
3. **Buying-group answers**: one short page per role (owner, ops, finance, IT): what they worry about and the
   honest answer (data stays theirs, a person approves every purchase, what the pilot costs: nothing).
4. **Follow-up messages** after a post interaction, a call and a demo, written in the same everyday language as the
   posts.

## 2. Website: more natural, human and premium

What the live site does well: clear structure, real ProcurePilot screens, honest about the pilot, both languages,
fast. What makes it feel generic or "AI-made" to a business buyer today:

| What a visitor sees | Why it feels generic | Proposed change |
|---|---|---|
| Headline "Powering Businesses with Intelligent Technologies" | Could be any software agency; it describes us, not the buyer's problem | Lead with what we do for a business, in plain words. Direction (wording to approve): *"We build the software your operations run on, and we stay to keep it running."* Arabic written natively, not translated |
| Company name as the hero headline, 3D logo animation | Big name + 3D object reads as a template; premium brands usually show less | Smaller wordmark, more space, one calm image or no image. Keep the 3D forge for one moment, not the first screen |
| No people anywhere | B2B buyers buy from people. No name, face or place makes us hard to trust | A short **founder section**: Dr. Yasser Hosny, photo, three sentences on why Iron Systems exists, where we work from (to confirm: not in our evidence yet) and who you'll work with |
| Services (4 cards) + AI capabilities (6 rows) + stack strip, all with tags | A lot of lists and chips; repeats itself; reads like a capability brochure | One section: the problems we solve, with ProcurePilot as the worked example. Fewer tags, more sentences |
| ProcurePilot as a product card | Our strongest proof is hidden in a tile | Its own case page (section 1) linked from the home page: "See something we built" |
| Contact form opens the email app | Extra step; unclear when we reply | "Book a 20-minute call" (calendar link) + WhatsApp + email, and "we reply within one working day" (only if we can keep it) |
| Dark theme, orange accents | Fine (owner decision: dark default). Premium comes from restraint, not colour | Keep the palette. More whitespace, fewer accent hits, larger and calmer type, slower and fewer animations |

Premium in B2B means **calm and specific**: fewer words but concrete ones, generous space, real material (our own
screens, our own video, a real photo of the founder), and no claims we can't show.

How it happens: the website lives in its own repo (`<workspace>/Website/iron-system-next`). Marketing writes the new
copy (EN + AR) and a page plan here; the website changes go through that repo's own process after Yasser approves.
The ProcurePilot landing page (`procurepilot.iron-sys.com`) is already specific and B2B; only the contact step
(call/WhatsApp) should match the company site.

## 3. Getting ProcurePilot's first customer

**Where we stand (2026-11-01):**

| Item | State |
|---|---|
| Pitch line, one-pager, DM script, demo video (all languages) | Done (PPS-16, PPS-18, PPS-14, video v2.5) |
| Prospect list | 14 companies in the Sales Pipeline (SP-2 to SP-15): 9 in Egypt, 5 in Saudi Arabia. **All 14 at "Target": nobody contacted yet** |
| Pilot agreement | In progress (PPS-15) |
| Pilot environment (separate from the demo/test system) | In progress (PPS-29). Real customer data can't go on the current hosted system |
| Company registration | In progress (PPS-11) |
| Outreach goal: 20 real conversations | Not started (PPS-19) |

The sales material is ready. **The gap is conversations.** A first B2B customer comes from Yasser talking to owners
and operations managers, not from posts. Conversations can start now: the pilot environment is only needed when
someone says yes, and getting from first message to a yes usually takes weeks.

**Proposed plan (4 weeks, starting this week):**

| Week | Outreach (Yasser) | Marketing support (Claude) | Delivery (team) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Write down 20 people you know who own or run, or can introduce you to, multi-branch restaurants in Egypt. Message 5 a day with the diagnostic question from the DM script | Add those names as SP leads; personalised first message for each; follow-up messages | Finish the pilot agreement (PPS-15) |
| 2 | Calls with whoever answers; show the 2-minute demo on the phone; ask for an intro to one more owner | Founding-customer offer sheet; buying-group answers | Pilot environment (PPS-29) |
| 3 | Visit the 2 or 3 warmest prospects in person; ask the qualification questions | Case page and new home-page copy (if approved) | Onboarding runbook (PPS-21) |
| 4 | Offer the free 14-day pilot to the best qualified prospect | Prepare their week-0 baseline sheet | Pilot environment tested and ready |

Focus choices behind it:

- **Egypt first for customer #1.** We're there, can visit in person, pricing and the pilot terms are in EGP, and
  the company is being registered there. Saudi leads stay in the pipeline for after the first case study.
- **Mid-size groups (2 to 15 branches) before big holdings.** The list has large names (Raya, Zooba); they're slow
  to decide. A 4-branch group whose owner answers WhatsApp is a better first customer. Add more mid-size groups
  from the personal-network list.
- **Warm before cold.** An introduction from someone they know gets an answer far more often than a cold DM.
- **Founding-customer offer as the hook**: "We're taking 3 founding customers", which is true and gives a reason
  to decide now.
- **Don't put a prospect's real quotes into the current hosted system.** If someone wants to "try it on our
  quotes" before the pilot environment is ready, show the demo instead, or do the comparison with them in a
  spreadsheet. Their data goes in only after the pilot environment is live and the agreement is signed.

Measures (weekly, 15 minutes, in the SP board): messages sent, conversations at "Talked", qualified, pilot offered.
Target from the GTM plan: 20 at "Talked", then one pilot.
